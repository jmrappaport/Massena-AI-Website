What an AI data center means for Massena

Massena makes clean hydropower that local industry no longer uses. AI data centers need that kind of power. Here is what a data center would and would not do here, and what the Town can require.

Why would a data center come to Massena?

Data centers are built where the power is. Across most of the U.S., that means building a gas-fired plant right next to the data center. Massena offers something rarer.

Power and energy

Clean hydropower that is already here

The St. Lawrence makes low-cost renewable power. When local industry closed or cut back, more than a gigawatt of that power was left without a user. Many data center companies still aim to be carbon neutral and want exactly this.

Infrastructure

Fiber to connect it

Places with both renewable power and enough fiber-optic lines are hard to find. Massena has both.

Climate

Cool weather helps

North Country winters mean less energy spent on cooling. It is a small bonus, not the main reason.

What would it bring to town?

An AI data center is a long-term, high-investment building. Here is what that can mean for jobs, taxes and the community. These are estimates. Exact numbers depend on the final project.

Jobs and economy

This is not a crypto mine

Massena already has some crypto mining sites. An AI data center is a very different building, with far more equipment, investment and skilled staff. Pay tends to follow the investment.

AI data center (Level 3)$11,000,000 per MW
Crypto mining site$300,000 per MW

Source: ZeroC Data Centers, written comments to the Massena Town Board, Jan. 22, 2026

Jobs and economy

Built for decades

Data center companies are looking for power contracts of 20 to 30 years or more. Every 3 to 5 years they upgrade their chips, which brings new sales tax and construction work each time.

Source: ZeroC discussions with large cloud companies and more than a dozen data center builders, 2025–2026

Community benefit

A fund the Town controls

A charge of 2 mills (two-tenths of a cent) per unit of data center power could raise $10 to $20 million a year. The Town Board would decide how to use it, such as for health care, a clinic, or cleanup led by Akwesasne.

Source: ZeroC proposal to the Massena Town Board, Jan. 22, 2026. Not yet adopted.

Things to watch

  • Construction traffic. Trucks on local roads during the build, and how routes would be managed.
  • Noise. Cooling fans and equipment run day and night. Setbacks and noise limits belong in the local law.
  • Backup generators. Data centers keep backup power for outages. These are tested on a schedule and fall under state emissions permits.
  • How many jobs. Pay is high, but the number of permanent jobs depends on the size of the project.
  • Water. Closed-loop cooling uses little water, but it should be written into the law, not just promised.

Source: to be added from project noise, traffic and permit studies as they become available

Your questions, answered

These came up at the Town’s public hearing on data center zoning on January 21, 2026. Where the Town can put a rule into law, we say so.

  • Power and energy
  • Jobs and economy
  • Land, water and environment
Power and energy

Will my electric bill go up?

A data center here would use power from different circuits than the ones that serve Massena homes and businesses. More than a gigawatt of power that used to go to local industry was left unused. That is the power a data center would buy, not the supply to your house.

Source: ZeroC Data Centers, written comments to the Massena Town Board, Jan. 22, 2026

Land, water and environment

Will it pull water from the St. Lawrence or the Grasse?

Early data center designs did ask for huge amounts of water, sometimes around 2,000 gallons per minute. That would have meant drawing from the rivers. In the past year, the industry has moved to closed-loop cooling, which recirculates the same water instead of drawing a constant supply.

What the local law can require

Closed-loop cooling. Any project wanting a different design would have to apply for a variance. The state Department of Environmental Conservation (DEC) already permits and enforces water use, and the law can say so.

Source: ZeroC Data Centers, written comments to the Massena Town Board, Jan. 22, 2026

Land, water and environment

Will it burn fossil fuels or add carbon pollution?

Clean hydropower is the reason data centers would come here. Elsewhere in the U.S., the most common choice is to build a gas plant next to the data center, which does produce carbon dioxide. Data centers coming to Massena have no plans for that.

What the local law can require

No carbon-based power generation on site, other than backup power. Because local power is very stable, backup systems would rarely run. DEC emissions rules apply either way.

Source: ZeroC Data Centers, written comments to the Massena Town Board, Jan. 22, 2026

Jobs and economy

Isn’t this just more crypto mining?

No. AI data centers cost about 37 times more to build per megawatt than crypto mines, and they employ skilled, well-paid staff. We expect most crypto mines to close within a year or two as mining becomes less profitable.

What the local law can require

The zoning can say “non-crypto” data centers only. Projects interested in Massena are not affected by that rule.

Source: ZeroC Data Centers, written comments to the Massena Town Board, Jan. 22, 2026

Jobs and economy

Will it be gone in five years?

That is unlikely given the size of the investment. The five-year idea may come from the chip upgrade cycle. Every 3 to 5 years, AI data centers swap in newer, more efficient chips. The building and the jobs stay. Companies are seeking power contracts of 20 to 30 years at a minimum.

Source: ZeroC discussions with large cloud companies and more than a dozen data center builders, 2025–2026

Jobs and economy

Are data centers targeting struggling communities?

No. The reason to build here is the hydropower that is already made here and no longer used. The cool climate is a small bonus. The jobs a Level 3 AI data center brings average about $90,000 a year.

Source: ZeroC Data Centers, written comments to the Massena Town Board, Jan. 22, 2026

Land, water and environment

Who pays for cleanup from past industry?

The harm done to Akwesasne by past aluminum industry is real. A new industry can’t take on the full cost of cleaning up after an old one, but it can help pay for the community’s priorities.

What the Town can set up

A 2-mill charge (two-tenths of a cent) on data center power. That could raise $10 to $20 million a year for the community. The Town Board would decide how it is spent, such as for health care, building a clinic, or cleanup managed by Akwesasne. A steady fund like this could also support borrowing for $200 to $300 million projects.

Source: ZeroC proposal to the Massena Town Board, Jan. 22, 2026. Not yet adopted.

Land, water and environment

Is it safe for our health?

A doctor at the hearing pointed to Montreal as a community with better health. Montreal is also home to several large data centers run by Vantage Data Centers, which says it has seen no health risks from them. Given what this region has been through, it is hard to name a safer kind of industry. We are organizing a tour so residents can see a modern data center for themselves.

Source: ZeroC conversation with Vantage Data Centers, reported to the Massena Town Board, Jan. 22, 2026

What we told the Town Board

On January 21, 2026, the Town held a public hearing on changing the zoning law to allow data centers. The next day, ZeroC Data Centers sent the Board a written response to the concerns raised. In it, we said we would support these rules in the law.

Read the full letter (PDF)

ZeroC Data Centers to the Massena Town Board, January 22, 2026

Rules we support in the local law

  • Closed-loop cooling only

    Other designs would need a variance. Water use stays under DEC permits.

  • Limited CO2 power plants on site

    Backup power for outages only. Massena’s hydro-fed grid is highly reliable, so backup systems would rarely need to run.

  • No crypto mining

    Zoning for “non-crypto” data centers.

  • Community Investment Fund

    A 2-mill charge on data center power, about $10–20 million a year, spent as the Town Board decides.

What we offered to do next

  • Meet with Akwesasne leadership to talk through concerns about modern data centers.
  • Take about a dozen residents from Massena and Akwesasne on a guided tour of a modern data center. We would cover travel and lodging.
